Have you ever felt an immediate connection with someone at work who, on paper, seems nothing like you?

That "click" moment happens when we discover unexpected similarities with colleagues — and it's at the heart of what creates genuine belonging in organizations.

My research revealed something fascinating: women, people of color, and employees at small/mid-sized companies feel significantly more engaged when they perceive their manager to be "like them." This "perception of similarity" drives engagement in powerful ways.

Connection runs deeper than what we can see.

This perception of similarity extends far beyond visible identity markers. It might emerge when:

- You discover you both attended the same university

- You learn you share a passion for the same hobby

- You find out you grew up in similar circumstances

- You realize you hold similar values about family

I've witnessed this repeatedly in my client work. Organizations rightfully focus on representation yet sometimes overlook creating spaces where people can share stories and discover unexpected connections.

Representation absolutely matters. People of color are exhausted having to blaze trails. Seeing people who look like you in leadership positions creates vital pathways forward.

AND when we broaden how people discover commonalities, we multiply the opportunities for authentic belonging.

Leaders who intentionally create opportunities for storytelling and relationship-building see remarkable results. Those small moments of "me too!" build the foundation for trust, psychological safety, and genuine engagement.

What unexpected connection have you discovered with a colleague that strengthened your working relationship?
