Why do we sometimes trust strangers more than people who actually know our lives?

This question haunts me as I revisit "Strangers in Their Own Land" by Arlie Russell Hochschild.

The book examines Trump's unexpected connection with voters whose lives couldn't be more different from his. But I'm not here to talk politics.

I'm obsessed with the connection puzzle:

→ How does a billionaire resonate with working-class voters?

→ Why do people feel "seen" by someone who's never lived their reality?

→ What creates trust across seemingly impossible divides?

When things feel out of control, I dive into understanding. Right now, I'm trying to crack this code of unlikely connections.

The answer could transform how we build authentic relationships in our divided world.

When have you felt deeply understood by someone who shouldn't "get" you at all?
