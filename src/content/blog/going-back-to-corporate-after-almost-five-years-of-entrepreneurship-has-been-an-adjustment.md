Going back to corporate after almost five years of entrepreneurship has been an adjustment.

I absolutely love where I am. The people are amazing, the work is exciting, and I can see how we as an organization can make a meaningful impact. But the transition from structuring my own time to back-to-back meetings and working nights to catch up? That’s been tough.

I’ve had to get really honest with myself about something: in past corporate roles, I was okay putting myself in second or third place when it came to time management and self-care. I’m not okay with that anymore.

The entrepreneurial freedom I miss most isn’t the flexibility to take a vacation whenever I want (though that was nice). It’s those small moments - being able to block off 30 minutes to do a couple of errands, or just play a game on my phone (Homescapes, anyone?) to clear my head between tasks.

But here’s what I’ve realized: going back to my values helps me navigate this constant internal tug-of-war. The feeling of connection I get from working with smart, interesting people and being able to create cultures of belonging outweighs the flexibility I gave up.

It’s about being more intentional with self-care and more present in meetings instead of thinking about my growing task list. That’s on me to figure out.

This weekend’s plan? Bad television, laundry, and remembering that sometimes the best part of adulting is doing absolutely nothing exciting on your couch.

For anyone else juggling multiple versions of success - corporate, entrepreneurial, or otherwise - remember that going back to your values can be your north star when everything else feels like chaos.
