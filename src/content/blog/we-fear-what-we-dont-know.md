We fear what we don't know.

I experienced this firsthand while traveling alone in Spain. In Madrid, I signed up for a cooking class with a local chef. What was supposed to be a three-hour market and cooking experience turned into a six-hour connection that crossed cultures and languages.

As we shopped at Mercado de la Paz and cooked together, our conversation deepened. We discovered a shared experience as older women navigating a world that often makes us feel invisible. I shared an expression with her that resonated deeply: "You learn to recognize the fucks you give and the fucks you don't."

She had never heard this phrase before and immediately wrote it down, excited to use it herself. In that moment, two women from different parts of the world connected over a universal feeling that transcended our surface differences.

This encounter taught me something I've carried throughout my career: When people have limited exposure to difference, assumptions fill the void. But those assumptions start to crumble with genuine conversation.

The workplace application is clear. As leaders, we can:

→ Create consistent opportunities for team members to share beyond surface-level identities.

→ Model curiosity instead of judgment.

→ Focus conversations on values and experiences that connect us.

When we build these bridges of understanding, we don't erase our uniqueness — we complement it by helping people see each other more fully.

The result? Teams that collaborate more effectively because they've moved past assumptions to actual understanding.
