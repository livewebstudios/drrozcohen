Recently heard Leslie Jones suggest all dating apps should merge into one truthfully named service: "What's Left." I found this hilarious because it's painfully accurate (and if we ever meet, I am happy to share a glass of wine and some of the funniest/oddest/most bizarre stories

My dating criteria have simplified over the years:

1. Do you have a pulse?

1. Did you NOT vote for Trump?

1. Do you dislike Burning Man? (I simply don't understand the appeal of chaos in a desert!)

With all that as context, I've been thinking about how we've been conditioned to believe that romantic relationships are the pinnacle of human connection. I've come to value my non-romantic relationships deeply – these women are my soulmates who’ve seen me through every life transition for almost 20 years.

True connection means someone asks about your day and genuinely listens to your answer. It means being curious about each other's lives – not just during the honeymoon phase, but consistently.

As I've gotten older, I've embraced the "walkaway factor." I don't need someone to complete my life – they need to enhance it. If they don't, I'm secure enough to say "this isn't working for me" and move on.

I’m curious: has your perspective on relationships also changed as you've gotten older?
