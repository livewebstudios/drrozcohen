I was talking about my PhD research on a podcast recently, and it brought back something fundamental about engagement that so many organizations overlook.

When it comes to employee engagement, we're not dealing with one monolithic concept – we're actually looking at THREE distinct dimensions:

1. The "I think" dimension (positive cognitive engagement)

1. The "I feel" dimension (positive affective engagement)

1. The "I belong" dimension (shared social engagement)

Here's what fascinates me — my research showed that inclusive leadership behaviors directly impact the "feel" and "connect" dimensions, but barely touch the "think" dimension.

This changes everything about how we approach engagement.

Your hiring process needs to target candidates with the right cognitive engagement ("I think") for your organization. Then your managers need to develop specific inclusive leadership behaviors to influence how people feel about their work and how connected they are to their colleagues.

I see this play out with my clients constantly. When companies struggle with engagement, they often miss this fundamental reality: leadership training won't fix cognitive engagement issues. And hiring practices alone won't solve emotional or social engagement problems.

This insight is exactly why I developed the TAMEE model (Talent Acquisition Model for Employee Engagement) - to help organizations use the right tools for each dimension of engagement.

Getting specific about which dimension we're targeting makes all the difference. It allows organizations to make real, measurable progress on building cultures where people genuinely belong.
