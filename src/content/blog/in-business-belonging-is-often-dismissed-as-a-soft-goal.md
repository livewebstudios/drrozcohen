In business, belonging is often dismissed as a "soft goal." But let me tell you why that's wrong.

My new role at [Laird Norton Wetherby](https://www.linkedin.com/company/lnwadvisors) came about because leadership recognized a crucial truth: when people feel connected and belong to a group, they are more committed. This isn't just feel-good HR talk – it's backed by research.

People who feel connected to each other or to a bigger picture demonstrate:

- Greater loyalty

- Increased sustainability

- Better feelings about their work

- Better feelings about themselves

When we experience belonging, we see ourselves reflected in the bigger culture. That reflection matters to performance.

Yes, there's a fear right now about discussing belonging because it feels adjacent to DEI. But here's the hard business truth: connection and belonging drive measurable outcomes.

The tricky part isn't proving belonging's value – it's navigating the current climate where these concepts are increasingly politicized. As practitioners, we know belonging is valuable, but there's hesitancy to highlight it.

What we need isn't one grand kumbaya moment, but people making hard decisions within their spheres of influence. Small pockets of people standing up for what matters. That's how real change happens.

Have you seen the relationship between belonging and hard business outcomes in your organization? I'd love to hear examples.
