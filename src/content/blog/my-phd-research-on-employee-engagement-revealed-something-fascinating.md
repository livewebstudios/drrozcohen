My PhD research on employee engagement revealed something fascinating: there are three distinct types of engagement that organizations should understand.

1. Positive cognitive engagement (the "I think")
2. Positive affective engagement (the "I feel")
3. Shared social engagement (the "I am connected")

Here's the key insight:

💡Inclusive leadership behaviors directly impact the "I feel" and "I am connected" aspects of engagement, but have no effect on the "I think" (cognitive) engagement.

This means your managers can't motivate internal drive through their behaviors alone. That cognitive piece needs to be present from the start.

That's why I developed TAMEE (Talent Acquisition Model for Employee Engagement): a framework showing how organizations can influence cognitive engagement through hiring processes, while developing the other forms of engagement through inclusive leadership practices.

The takeaway? When building engaged teams, focus on finding people with the right cognitive fit in your hiring process, then train your managers to build connection and belonging after they're onboard.
