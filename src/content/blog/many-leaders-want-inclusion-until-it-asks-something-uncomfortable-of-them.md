Many leaders want inclusion until it asks something uncomfortable of them. And in today's polarized environment, this is more evident than ever.

When I think about brave leadership around inclusion, I don't focus on grand gestures. The bravery I've witnessed comes from people making statements that challenge what's being amplified in media conversations.

We don't need to look far for examples. Consider Cory Booker standing firm during his filibuster. Or, as I saw in a recent video, a veteran challenging the younger generation on science versus ideology around the topic of abortion. These aren't grandiose movements – they're individuals speaking their truth.

What's more important – and braver – are the small decisions people make within their spheres of influence:

- The manager who speaks up in a contentious meeting

- The colleague who asks a challenging question

- The leader who creates space for uncomfortable but necessary conversations

I don't believe in waiting for one kumbaya moment to bring everyone together. That's unrealistic. What matters are the everyday decisions that influence others to think, "If they did it, maybe I can challenge this too."

True courage in leadership isn't about heroic moments. It's about standing up for what matters in your sphere, however small it might seem. These acts within spheres of influence are far more impactful than waiting for grand societal shifts.
