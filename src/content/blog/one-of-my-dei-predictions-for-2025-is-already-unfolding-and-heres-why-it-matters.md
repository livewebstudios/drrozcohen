One of my DEI predictions for 2025 is already unfolding, and here's why it matters.

The latest Anti-Defamation League (ADL) Global 100 survey reveals a troubling reality: 46% of people worldwide hold antisemitic views.

This isn't just about statistics. It's a wake-up call.

The numbers are stark: 20% of survey respondents haven't heard about the Holocaust, while less than half recognize its historical accuracy. In certain regions, that recognition drops even lower.

These findings arrive at a critical moment when many organizations are rethinking their approach to inclusion and belonging. What we're seeing isn't just a concerning trend– it directly impacts our ability to create workplaces where everyone feels valued and connected.

The question isn't whether to address this. The question is: how?

As leaders, our commitment to building cultures of belonging needs to stay strong, especially as prejudice rises globally.

We don’t need any more performative gestures or checking boxes. We need to do the real work to create workplaces where people truly belong. Agree?
