Anyone old enough to remember Loehmann’s? It was a higher end version of Nordstrom Rack with one very unique feature: the dressing room wasn’t individual stalls. It was one big communal room with mirrors everywhere and those gorgeous fluorescent lights that made everyone look… well, you know.

I was thinking about this the other day while online shopping with my morning coffee.

Walking into the dressing room was terrifying. Here you are, about to get undressed in front of complete strangers and stand in front of a wall of mirrors so you, and ALL of these women, can see you FROM EVERY ANGLE.

But here’s what would happen: You’d be standing there, criticizing yourself in the mirror while trying on clothes, and suddenly the woman across the room would say, “That looks amazing on you.” Others would chime in: “It does! That’s totally your color.” “You should wear that with this belt.” “That would be perfect for dinner out.”

In an instant, you became part of a community. These women, whom you’d never seen before and would likely never see again, were lifting each other up, offering feedback you didn’t know you needed but really wanted.

All of us together in our most vulnerable state, supporting each other.

Now I sit here online shopping in isolation, and I realize something: I didn’t know I missed Loehmann’s until this very moment.

We’ve gained convenience, but we’ve lost something fundamental - those unexpected moments of human connection and support. The spontaneous community that forms when people are vulnerable together.

I think about this a lot in the work I do around creating cultures of belonging. Sometimes the most powerful connections happen in the spaces where we feel most exposed, most uncertain. When we create room for that vulnerability - even in professional settings - we open up possibilities for the kind of authentic support that actually changes how people feel about showing up.

What unexpected place have you found genuine connection and support?
